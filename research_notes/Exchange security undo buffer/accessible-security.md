# Accessible security for a bilingual family wallet

Research date: 7 November 2026. Audience: a Miami family sending support, including people who will not learn wallet jargon. Source claims are under Cited Findings. Design inferences are under Inferences and are not quotations from those sources.

Scope limit used throughout: the CFPB remittance rule applies to remittance transfer providers sending more than $15 from a U.S. consumer to a person or company in another country. A company that provided 500 or fewer transfers in the prior calendar year and 500 or fewer in the current year is not a provider, and federal law then does not require these protections. These notes use the rule as a disclosure and language checklist. They do not decide whether a particular family wallet is a provider. — [CFPB, “What is a remittance transfer and what are my rights?” last reviewed 3 October 2024](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-remittance-transfer-and-what-are-my-rights-en-1161/)

## What do remittance and consumer-finance usability sources say about confirmation, recipient verification, fee disclosure, and status language?

### Takeaway

Before the sender pays, official U.S. remittance disclosures put the amount they pay, the provider’s fees and taxes, the total, the exchange rate, covered third-party fees, and the amount the recipient is expected to get in one grouped block, and they say the sender is not yet committed. A second, later fact is when the money will be available. Usability research says a confirmation only works if it restates the specific person, amount, and consequence, and payment-system research says the name and destination should be checked before money moves, especially when the payment cannot be pulled back.

### Cited Findings

- A pre-payment disclosure is given after the sender requests the transfer and before the sender pays. It must show, as applicable and with the rule’s terms or substantially similar terms: “Transfer Amount,” provider “Transfer Fees” and “Transfer Taxes,” “Total,” “Exchange Rate” (rounded consistently to between two and four decimal places), covered third-party “Other Fees,” and “Total to Recipient.” The provider must also state that non-covered third-party fees or foreign taxes may reduce what the recipient gets. The sender is not obligated to complete the transfer after seeing this disclosure, and a provider must give a new accurate disclosure if the sender leaves and the rate or fees change. — [12 CFR 1005.31(b), Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/frrs/regulations/section-100531-disclosures.htm); [CFPB Small Entity Compliance Guide](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_remittance-transfers_small-entity-compliance-guide.pdf)
- The provider may not disclose the exchange rate as floating, unknown, or to be determined. Calculations use the actual rate; only the figure shown to the sender is rounded. Estimates are allowed only in specified cases and must be identifiable as estimates. — [CFPB Small Entity Compliance Guide](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_remittance-transfers_small-entity-compliance-guide.pdf); [CFPB consumer rights page](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-remittance-transfer-and-what-are-my-rights-en-1161/)
- “Date Available,” meaning the date in the foreign country when funds will be available to the designated recipient, is a receipt disclosure, not part of the pre-payment list. The provider may disclose the latest date and add that funds “may be available sooner.” — [12 CFR 1005.31(b)(2)](https://www.federalreserve.gov/frrs/regulations/section-100531-disclosures.htm); [CFPB Small Entity Compliance Guide](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_remittance-transfers_small-entity-compliance-guide.pdf)
- The receipt also states the cancellation right and error rights. The English model receipt says: “You can cancel for a full refund within 30 minutes of payment, unless the funds have been picked up or deposited.” It also says the sender can dispute errors within 180 days. The model pre-payment form is labeled “NOT A RECEIPT.” — [CFPB model form A-31](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/201408_cfpb_model-form_remittance-rule-a-31.pdf); [CFPB model form A-30(a)](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/201304_cfpb_model-form_remittance-rule-a-30-a.pdf)
- Disclosures must be clear and conspicuous, generally in writing and retainable. Required pre-payment items must be grouped, in close proximity, segregated from other information, and generally in at least 8-point type. A provider may rely on the model form for the format. Eight-point type is a paper-disclosure minimum in the rule, not a mobile readability target. — [CFPB Small Entity Compliance Guide, format section](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_remittance-transfers_small-entity-compliance-guide.pdf)
- Disclosures are always in English. For most transactions the provider must also give them in each foreign language principally used to advertise, solicit, or market remittance services at the office where the sender conducts the transfer or asserts an error, or instead in the language the sender actually used if that language is one the provider principally uses to market at that office. Foreign-language disclosures must accurately translate the required terms, language, and notices. — [CFPB Small Entity Compliance Guide, § 4.1.5](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_remittance-transfers_small-entity-compliance-guide.pdf); [CFPB examination procedures, comment 31(b)-2](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_remittance-transfer-examination-procedures.pdf); [CFPB consumer rights page](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-remittance-transfer-and-what-are-my-rights-en-1161/)
- Official Spanish is not one fixed glossary. The Spanish model receipt uses “Cantidad de Envío,” “Cargos por Envío,” “Impuestos de Envío,” “Tasa de Cambio,” “Otros Cargos por Envío,” “Total al Destinatario,” and “Fecha Disponible,” and its disclaimer uses “beneficiario” and “comisiones.” The CFPB’s Spanish consumer brochure uses the shorter set “Cantidad,” “Cargos,” “Impuestos,” “Tasa de cambio,” “Otros cargos,” and “TOTAL PARA EL DESTINATARIO,” and says “destinatario” and “tarifas.” The long Spanish error-and-cancellation model uses “envío de dinero,” “destinatario,” and “tarifas o gastos.” — [Model form A-39](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/201304_cfpb_model-form_remittance-rule-a-39.pdf); [CFPB brochure “Envíe dinero al extranjero con más confianza”](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_send-money-abroad_es.pdf); [Model form A-41](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/201304_cfpb_model-form_remittance-rule-a-41.pdf)
- The English model receipt uses “Confirmation Code” for the receipt identifier. The Spanish model uses “Código de Confirmación” for that same identifier. In these forms, “confirmation” is a reference number, not a statement that money has arrived. — [Model form A-31](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/201408_cfpb_model-form_remittance-rule-a-31.pdf); [Model form A-39](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/201304_cfpb_model-form_remittance-rule-a-39.pdf)
- If the sender gave an incorrect account number or recipient-institution identifier and the funds were deposited in the wrong account, the provider may be excused from refunding or resending only when it can show the number was wrong, it gave the sender notice that the transfer amount could be lost, it used prompt and reasonable efforts to get the funds back, and, for an institution identifier, it had used reasonable means to verify that identifier. For other errors caused by incorrect or insufficient information from the sender, the provider must refund the principal, or resend if the sender chooses that before the refund, and may deduct only covered third-party fees and taxes actually imposed, not its own fees. — [CFPB Small Entity Compliance Guide, § 5.2.3 and 12 CFR 1005.33(h)](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_remittance-transfers_small-entity-compliance-guide.pdf)
- A November 2025 CPMI staff brief describes payment pre-validation as checking accuracy, validity, and completeness before a cross-border payment is initiated. Confirmation of payee, also called verification of payee, matches at least the name and an account identifier, or a proxy such as a phone number, before the payer confirms and funds move. The brief says this matters especially for fast payments that are irrevocable once processed. It also says comprehensive data attributing fraud reduction to pre-validation are limited; it reports, via Pay.UK (2025b), that certain UK end-user fraud losses fell 20–40% since confirmation of payee, and, via UK Finance (2025), that 2024 authorized-push-payment losses fell 2% to just over £450 million and the number of cases fell 20%. Those underlying reports were not opened for this note. The brief says cross-border pre-validation remains limited, that user education is still required against social engineering, and that immediate feedback on details, expected time, and charges builds trust. The brief states that its views are the authors’, not necessarily those of the BIS or CPMI. — [CPMI Brief No. 9, November 2025](https://www.bis.org/publications/cpmi-brief-9-safety-and-efficiency-through-payment-pre-validation-spotting-issues-money-moves.pdf)
- Nielsen Norman Group’s confirmation-dialog guidance: use a confirmation before serious or hard-to-undo consequences; do not use one for routine actions, because people stop reading; restate the request and the effect in specifics rather than “Are you sure?”; label buttons with the outcome (“Delete file” / “Keep file”), not Yes/No; do not default the dangerous answer; keep the dialog short and put extra detail behind a disclosure; reserve a nonstandard confirmation, such as typing a word, for rare high-risk actions, because frequent use becomes automatic. The same group says undo is preferable when the product can offer it, and that a form error should be shown next to the field rather than only in a modal, so the person can fix it while looking at the input. A separate NN/g article notes that some banks add an intrusive step, such as re-entering a password, before a wire, and that a confirmation people see constantly fails even if one was shown. — [Confirmation Dialogs Can Prevent User Errors](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/confirmation-dialog/); [Preventing User Errors: Avoiding Conscious Mistakes](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/user-mistakes/); [Modal and Nonmodal Dialogs](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/modal-nonmodal-dialog/); [Error Prevention and the Hawaiian missile alert](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/error-prevention/)
- Product help, used only as language comparison: Wise tells the sender to match the on-screen status to what they can still do. Before payment, “Cancel transfer” is available. While the transfer “is being processed,” Wise says the cancel button is gone because it is too late to cancel it yourself, staff might still catch it but cannot guarantee that, and once it is “complete” the money is in the recipient’s banking system and Wise has no way to bring it back. If details are wrong, Wise says the recipient bank will most likely reject it, or, if it reached the wrong account, the sender must ask that person to return it. Wise’s tracker uses “Your money’s being processed,” “Money received” (Wise has it and is converting), and “Transfer Sent” (sent to the recipient’s bank, which may still take working days). — [Wise, “How do I cancel my transfer?”](https://wise.com/help/articles/2971625/how-do-i-cancel-my-transfer); [Wise, “How do I check my transfer’s status?”](https://wise.com/help/articles/2452305/how-do-i-check-my-transfers-status)
- Western Union’s UK help page, not a U.S. disclosure, separates “In progress” (processed and on its way), “Available” (ready for pickup, not yet collected), “Received” (picked up), “Paid” or “Delivered” (credited to a wallet or bank), “On hold” (“taking us longer than usual because we are verifying a few things”), “Cancelled,” and “Refunded.” It says no action is needed while a transfer stays in progress, and to contact them if that status lasts more than 48 hours. The same page says a cancelled transfer’s money is “usually refunded within 7 business days” and, elsewhere, within 5–7 business days from when the refund was processed. — [Western Union UK, transfer status FAQ](https://www.westernunion.com/gb/en/frequently-asked-questions/faq-track-your-transfer.html)
- Remitly’s U.S. error-resolution notice tracks the regulation: contact within 180 days of the promised availability date; investigation within 90 days; results within three business days after the investigation; cancel for a full refund, including fees, before the funds are deposited or collected; refund within three business days of the cancel request if not already picked up or deposited. Remitly’s app help, updated 8 October 2026, says to read the latest status together with any instruction, that an expected delivery time is an estimate and not a promise, and that “Ready for pickup” means the money can be collected and does not mean it has been collected. If the app shows nothing for the sender to do, the instruction is to wait. — [Remitly U.S. error and cancellation notice](https://www.remitly.com/pdf/en/us/a83c42c5-a986-44d1-aede-e944cffbda8d); [Remitly, “How to Track Your Money Transfer,” 7–8 October 2026](https://www.remitly.com/blog/money-transfer/how-to-track-your-money-transfer-with-the-remitly-app/)
- A September 2024 CFPB proposal would narrowly change where a sender is told to take unresolved problems or complaints (the state licensing agency and the CFPB), and the related model language. The Spring 2025 Unified Agenda listed a final rule for December 2025. The 2026 Unified Agenda still lists RIN 3170-AB28 at the final-rule stage. This note did not retrieve a Federal Register final rule. The agenda abstract does not describe a change to the amount, fee, exchange-rate, or date-available disclosures. — [CFPB rule page, 20 September 2024](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/rules-under-development/remittance-transfers-under-the-electronic-fund-transfer-act-regulation-e/); [OIRA, RIN 3170-AB28, Spring 2025](https://www.reginfo.gov/public/do/eAgendaViewRule?RIN=3170-AB28&pubId=202504); [OIRA agency rule list, 2026](https://www.reginfo.gov/public/do/eAgendaMain?agencyCd=3170&currentPub=true&operation=OPERATION_GET_AGENCY_RULE_LIST)

### Inferences

- The screen a beginner needs before sending is the pre-payment block, not a transaction hash: how much leaves the sender, fees and taxes, total, rate, how much the recipient is expected to get, who and where it is going, and the date it should be available. Label that screen as not yet sent. Keep “date available” off the word “sent.”
- Show a mismatch, a close match, or “could not check” in a sentence before the sender can commit, and say whether money can be recovered if they continue. That follows the pre-validation brief and the account-number warning in Regulation E. It is not a claim that a family wallet already has a payee-matching network.
- One specific review beats repeated “Are you sure?” dialogs. The button should name the person and the amount the sender pays. Do not copy Wise, Western Union, or Remitly wording as the product’s specification; use it to see which distinctions non-experts already meet in the market: processing is not delivered, ready for pickup is not collected, and complete means the sender can no longer pull it back.
- Pick one Spanish pair and keep it. “Destinatario” plus “cargos,” or the model receipt’s “Cantidad de Envío” set, both appear in CFPB materials. Mixing “beneficiario,” “comisiones,” and “tarifas” on one screen recreates the variation across official forms. Do not use “confirmado” alone for a blockchain step, because the official Spanish receipt already uses “Código de Confirmación” for a reference number.
- Do not treat the rule’s 8-point minimum as the type size on a phone. It is a floor for paper disclosures. Phone size and reflow are covered by WCAG, below.

### Gaps

- No Nielsen Norman Group study of remittance, Spanish-English senders, or crypto payout was found in this pass.
- The UK fraud percentages are second-hand, through the November 2025 CPMI staff brief. Pay.UK (2025b) and UK Finance (2025) were not opened.
- This note does not determine whether any named company’s help text matches that company’s Regulation E obligation. Wise’s operational cutoff at “being processed” is earlier and less certain than the statute’s 30-minute right; the legal mapping was not researched.
- No Federal Register final rule for the 2024 disclosure proposal was retrieved. The 2026 agenda still shows final-rule stage.

## How should an app separate three statuses for a non-expert: blockchain confirmed, payout provider accepted, and local money paid out?

### Takeaway

Consumer remittance language already separates “on the way,” “ready to collect,” and “paid or picked up.” Developer guidance for stablecoin remittances says the same split in operational form: an on-chain transfer, a payout provider’s acceptance, and local payout are different evidence, and a settled stablecoin leg is not delivery of local money. For the sender, the on-chain step is also the step official crypto warnings say cannot be stopped or reversed.

### Cited Findings

- The CFPB receipt’s “Date Available” is the date funds will be available to the designated recipient in the foreign country. The cancellation right ends, even inside 30 minutes, once the funds have been picked up by the designated recipient or deposited into an account of the designated recipient. Availability, pickup, and deposit are different events from the moment the sender pays. — [12 CFR 1005.31(b)(2)](https://www.federalreserve.gov/frrs/regulations/section-100531-disclosures.htm); [12 CFR 1005.34](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1005/subpart-B/section-1005.34); [CFPB consumer rights page](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-remittance-transfer-and-what-are-my-rights-en-1161/)
- Wise’s sender-facing timeline has three different meanings after payment: money received and being converted, transfer sent to the recipient’s bank, and complete, at which point Wise says it cannot bring the money back. “Transfer Sent” still leaves delivery with the recipient’s bank and “can take a few working days.” — [Wise status help](https://wise.com/help/articles/2452305/how-do-i-check-my-transfers-status); [Wise cancellation help](https://wise.com/help/articles/2971625/how-do-i-cancel-my-transfer)
- Western Union’s UK status list separates in progress, available for pickup, and received, paid, or delivered. Remitly’s October 2026 help says ready for pickup is not collected. — [Western Union UK FAQ](https://www.westernunion.com/gb/en/frequently-asked-questions/faq-track-your-transfer.html); [Remitly, 8 October 2026](https://www.remitly.com/blog/money-transfer/how-to-track-your-money-transfer-with-the-remitly-app/)
- A Tempo developer guide, which is an integration document and not a consumer standard, says a stablecoin remittance can still pay out to a bank, wallet, or cash service. It tells implementers to keep sender funds accepted, stablecoins available, funds submitted on chain, destination funds received, and recipient payout complete as separate states. It says a single “complete” status hides where a delay needs intervention, and: “Don’t mark a fiat remittance delivered because the stablecoin leg settled.” It also says an accepted request, an on-chain transfer, and a completed local payout should have distinct meanings in the customer experience, and that the recipient does not have to understand stablecoins when the promised outcome is local currency. On-chain settlement proves the remittance is complete only when the promised outcome is the verified on-chain receipt itself. — [Tempo, “Remittance payment operations”](https://tempo.xyz/developers/docs/resources/guides/remittances)
- Polygon’s payments documentation, also a developer API rather than consumer copy, keeps inbound confirmation inside `processing` and says funds are not final until the transaction completes. It distinguishes `processing.cryptoOut` (submitted to the network), `processing.fiatOut` (submitted to the fiat rail), `processing.payoutHeld` (the provider accepted a fiat payout and is holding it), `processing.cashPickupReady` (code and location available), `completed.cashPickupCollected`, and `completed.cashPickupExpired`. — [Polygon, transaction lifecycle](https://docs.polygon.technology/payments/core-concepts/transaction-lifecycle); [Polygon, cash pickup](https://docs.polygon.technology/payments/guides/cash-pickup)
- Official warnings about the crypto leg, retrieved 7 November 2026: the CFTC says a transfer to a wallet address cannot be reversed, no matter the amount, and its digital-asset red-flag sheet says that once bitcoin is transferred on the blockchain it cannot be changed or stopped and typically cannot be recovered, that there are no charge-backs, and that all such transactions are final. FBI IC3 says cryptocurrency transactions are irrevocable. The CFPB’s November 2022 crypto complaint bulletin reports firms telling consumers that transfers are not reversible because of the crypto-asset. An archived CFPB consumer post says that if you pay with cryptocurrency you usually cannot get the money back unless the person you paid sends it back. These warnings are about the crypto transfer itself. They are not a description of a later cash pickup. — [CFTC, “Pause Before You Pay”](https://www.cftc.gov/LearnAndProtect/AdvisoriesAndArticles/ATM.html); [CFTC, “Curious About Crypto?”](https://www.cftc.gov/sites/default/files/LearnandProtect/DigitalAssetRedFlags.pdf); [FBI IC3, Cryptocurrency](https://www.ic3.gov/CrimeInfo/Cryptocurrency/); [CFPB Complaint Bulletin, November 2022](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_complaint-bulletin_crypto-assets_2022-11.pdf); [CFPB archive, scams targeting older adults](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/blog/how-to-prevent-and-report-scams-targeting-older-adults/)

### Inferences

- Show three sentences, in order, and never one word that covers all three. Suggested plain language, written for this note and not taken from a source:

  1. Recorded, not delivered: “This send is recorded and cannot be undone. [Name] does not have the money yet.” Spanish: “Este envío ya quedó registrado y no se puede deshacer. [Nombre] todavía no tiene el dinero.”
  2. Payout company accepted, not paid out: “The company that pays out locally has accepted it. They still have to deliver it to [bank or pickup]. You cannot cancel it yourself.” Spanish: “La empresa que entrega el dinero ya aceptó el envío. Todavía no está entregado a [banco o lugar de retiro]. Usted ya no puede cancelarlo por su cuenta.”
  3. Local money paid out: “Paid to [Name] at [place].” If the method is cash, split this into “Ready to collect” and “Collected.” Spanish: “Listo para retirar” and “Ya lo retiró [Nombre],” or “Depositado en la cuenta de [Nombre].”

- “Ready to collect” is not status 3. Remitly and Western Union both keep that step short of the recipient having the money. Using “paid out” for a pickup code would hide the limit the sources insist on.
- Status 1 is where irreversibility must be visible. Hiding it because the payout company might still fail, and might later return funds by a new transfer, would contradict the CFTC and IC3 statements. A later return is a new event. It does not make the recorded transfer reversible.
- Do not say “confirmed” or “confirmado” as the heading for status 1. On the CFPB Spanish receipt, confirmation is a code. Prefer “recorded” / “registrado,” and if a reference number is shown, label it “receipt number” / “número de recibo.”
- The recipient can be told only the local step (“the money is ready to pick up” / “el dinero está listo para retirar”) when that is the promised outcome. The sender still sees that the recorded step cannot be undone. Tempo’s note that the recipient need not understand stablecoins supports a plain local outcome. It does not support hiding finality from the sender.
- Do not copy Polygon’s `subStatus` strings or Tempo’s stage table into the interface. They are evidence that the three events are already distinct in payout systems.

### Gaps

- No U.S. consumer-agency page was found that already names these three statuses in one plain-language script for a stablecoin remittance.
- Polygon and Tempo pages are vendor developer docs. They describe those products’ state machines, not a legal definition of “paid.”
- This note did not verify, for any live corridor, how long status 2 usually lasts or what evidence a payout partner actually returns.

## What plain-language patterns exist for hold, delay, and cancel before a payment is final, including bilingual considerations for US families sending support?

### Takeaway

The durable pattern in the rule and in sender help is to say three things at once: whether the sender must act or wait, what has not happened yet, and the exact event after which the sender cannot pull the money back. For a crypto-funded send, that last event arrives when the transfer is recorded, which can be earlier than pickup or deposit. English remains mandatory in the remittance rule, and Spanish is added when it is a language the provider uses to market or to conduct the transfer. Both languages should carry the same limit.

### Cited Findings

- A sender’s Regulation E cancel request must reach the provider no later than 30 minutes after payment, must identify the sender and the transfer, and does not apply if the funds have already been picked up or deposited. The provider then refunds the total the sender provided, including fees and, where law allows, taxes, within three business days, at no extra cost. The sender does not have to wait at a store; they need a way to contact the provider inside the 30 minutes. Scheduled transfers set up at least three business days ahead follow a different cancellation rule under 12 CFR 1005.36(c). — [12 CFR 1005.34](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1005/subpart-B/section-1005.34); [CFPB Small Entity Compliance Guide, § 5.1](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_remittance-transfers_small-entity-compliance-guide.pdf)
- The long Spanish model says the sender can cancel and obtain a refund of all the money, including fees, by contacting the provider within 30 minutes of payment, and that the refund comes within three business days unless the funds were picked up or deposited: “a no ser que los fondos hayan sido recogidos o depositados en la cuenta del destinatario.” The short Spanish receipt compresses the same limit: “Puede cancelar el envío y recibir un reembolso total dentro de 30 minutos de haber realizado el pago, a no ser que los fondos hayan sido recogidos o depositados.” — [Model form A-41](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/201304_cfpb_model-form_remittance-rule-a-41.pdf); [Model form A-39](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/201304_cfpb_model-form_remittance-rule-a-39.pdf)
- The English short form is: “You can cancel for a full refund within 30 minutes of payment, unless the funds have been picked up or deposited.” — [Model form A-31](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/201408_cfpb_model-form_remittance-rule-a-31.pdf)
- Error rights are a different clock from cancellation. The sender has 180 days from the disclosed availability date to report an error. The provider investigates, generally within 90 days, and tells the sender the result within three business days after finishing. Some errors, including money that never arrives, can lead to a refund or a resend. The CFPB consumer page says to contact the company as soon as possible even though the window is 180 days. — [CFPB consumer rights page](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-remittance-transfer-and-what-are-my-rights-en-1161/); [Model form A-31](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/201408_cfpb_model-form_remittance-rule-a-31.pdf)
- Hold and delay language in market help, for comparison: Western Union UK says “On hold” means it is taking longer because they are verifying something, lists identity, other details, the sender’s bank, or waiting for information from the sender, and asks for accurate legal names. It separates that from “Cancelled” and from “Refunded.” Wise says a delay may be the sender’s bank, a request for more information by email, a mistake in recipient details, or a weekend or holiday, and tells the sender to watch for that email. Remitly says that if the transfer needs attention, follow the instruction in the app rather than guessing, and that after the sender responds the company may still need time to review. — [Western Union UK FAQ](https://www.westernunion.com/gb/en/frequently-asked-questions/faq-track-your-transfer.html); [Wise status help](https://wise.com/help/articles/2452305/how-do-i-check-my-transfers-status); [Remitly, 8 October 2026](https://www.remitly.com/blog/money-transfer/how-to-track-your-money-transfer-with-the-remitly-app/)
- WCAG 2.2 Success Criterion 3.1.2 (Level AA) requires that the human language of each passage or phrase be programmatically determinable, except proper names, technical terms, and words that have become part of the surrounding language. Success Criterion 3.1.5 (Level AAA), updated 28 June 2026, says that when a page uses more than one language, readability is calculated for each language that is at least 5% of the content and is used in full sentences, and the page is judged by the worse result. — [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/); [Understanding 3.1.5 Reading Level](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reading-level.html)

### Inferences

- Before anything is recorded, say the window in clock time and in events: “You can cancel until [time], or until this send is recorded, whichever comes first. After it is recorded, it cannot be undone.” Do not print the Regulation E sentence “30 minutes unless picked up or deposited” unless this product actually provides that right. A self-custody or instant on-chain send may be final in seconds, so copying the 30-minute sentence would overstate the protection.
- A hold screen should answer four questions in both languages: what is being checked, whether the money has been paid to the recipient, whether the sender must do something, and whether the recorded transfer can still be undone. If the crypto leg is already recorded, the hold may delay or block payout; it does not undo the recording. Say that.
- A delay is not a cancellation. Pair “This is later than the date we showed” with “It has not been cancelled” and with the current status from the three-status set. Remitly’s “the estimate is not a promise” and the CFPB phrase “may be available sooner” are the two directions of the same idea: the date is a bound, not a guarantee of the exact hour.
- If the only recovery left is “ask the person who received it,” say that in those words, as Wise does. Do not call that a refund.
- For a household that uses both languages, show English and Spanish together rather than detecting a single language and dropping the other. That matches the rule’s example of giving English and Spanish to a sender who conducts the transfer in Spanish, and it matches WCAG’s requirement to mark each passage’s language. Set the page language and mark each Spanish or English block so a screen reader uses the right voice.
- Keep the limit sentence equally specific in Spanish. A shorter, vaguer Spanish line (“ya se envió”) would hide the limit from the family member most likely to read Spanish. Use “no se puede deshacer” for the crypto recording, and reserve “recogido o depositado” for the local payout step, because that is what the CFPB Spanish models mean by those words.
- “Días hábiles” should be used only for a business-day count, as in the three-day refund clock. Do not translate a same-day on-chain recording as “días hábiles.”

### Gaps

- No user test with Miami Spanish-English families was found or run. The warning about “confirmado” is an inference from CFPB vocabulary, not a measured misreading.
- State money-transmitter rules that might add a longer cancel right were not researched. The CFPB consumer page notes that state law may add protections.
- Product pages do not define “on hold” for a transfer that is already recorded on a public network. The sentence that a hold does not undo a recording is an inference from the CFTC and IC3 finality statements plus the remittance distinction between delay and cancel.

## Which accessibility requirements matter on a phone: reading level, confirmation of amount and destination, error recovery?

### Takeaway

For a financial submission, WCAG 2.2 Level AA requires that the sender can reverse it, or that the input is checked and the sender can correct it, or that the sender can review, confirm, and correct it before it is final. A recorded crypto transfer fails the reversible option, so the review of amount and destination has to happen first and has to say that what follows cannot be undone. Errors have to be text, next to the field, with a way to fix them. Reading-level support is a AAA provision: if the text is harder than about nine years of school, provide a simpler version. Phone layout has to keep the amount and the recipient visible at 200% text size and at a narrow width, and status changes have to be announced, not shown by color alone.

### Cited Findings

- WCAG 2.2 Success Criterion 3.3.4 (Level AA), understanding page updated 16 September 2025: for a page that causes a financial transaction, at least one of these is true: the submission is reversible; data are checked for input errors and the user can correct them; or a mechanism is available to review, confirm, and correct the information before finalizing. The intent is to help people who may transpose numbers or hit the wrong control avoid a mistake that cannot be undone. The W3C’s order-confirmation example shows the items, quantities, address, and payment method for inspection, with a way to confirm or change them. Sufficient techniques include a stated time to amend or cancel (G164), review and correct before submitting (G98), and a checkbox in addition to the submit button (G155). — [Understanding 3.3.4](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/error-prevention-legal-financial-data)
- Success Criterion 3.3.1 (Level A): if an input error is automatically detected, the item is identified and the error is described in text. “Email is not valid” meets 3.3.1; “Please provide a valid email address in the format name@domain.com” also meets 3.3.3 Error Suggestion. Success Criterion 3.3.2 (Level A): labels or instructions are provided when content requires input. Association of those labels in markup is a separate requirement, 1.3.1. Success Criterion 3.3.3 (Level AA): if a correction is known, suggest it, unless the suggestion would undermine security. — [Understanding 3.3.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/error-identification); [Understanding 3.3.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/labels-or-instructions); [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)
- Success Criterion 3.1.5 (Level AAA): when text, after removing proper names and titles, requires reading ability beyond lower secondary education, provide supplemental content or a version that does not. Lower secondary is years 7–9 of school, about nine years total. Short, common words and short sentences are easier to decode. The criterion is not required for Level AA conformance. The understanding page points authors to the U.S. government’s plain-language guidance and to ISO 24495-1:2023. — [Understanding 3.1.5, updated 28 June 2026](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reading-level.html)
- Success Criterion 2.5.8 (Level AA): pointer targets are at least 24 by 24 CSS pixels, or they qualify for a spacing, equivalent, inline, user-agent, or essential exception. The understanding page says the research behind it was mostly about touchscreens, that 24 pixels is the minimum for all pointer types, and that important controls should be considered for the stricter 44 by 44 CSS pixel AAA target (2.5.5). Zoom does not change CSS pixel size, so zoom is not a way to meet the criterion. The point is to reduce accidental activation of a neighboring control. — [Understanding 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum)
- Success Criterion 1.4.4 (Level AA): text can be resized to 200% without loss of content or functionality, except captions and images of text. Success Criterion 1.4.10 (Level AA): content reflows at a width of 320 CSS pixels without requiring two-dimensional scrolling, except where a two-dimensional layout is essential. Success Criterion 1.4.3 (Level AA): text contrast is at least 4.5:1, or 3:1 for large text. Success Criterion 1.4.1 (Level A): color is not the only visual means of conveying information. Success Criterion 4.1.3 (Level AA): status messages, including success, waiting, progress, and errors, that do not take focus, must be programmatically determinable so assistive technology can present them. — [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/); [Understanding 4.1.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/status-messages)
- Visa’s mobile accessibility requirements, a company checklist mapped to WCAG and not a law, say that when input is collected across screens for a transaction, the user gets a summary before the final submit and can change it, and that an inline error highlights the field and places helper text next to it. — [Visa Product Design System, mobile interaction](https://design.visa.com/global-accessibility-requirements/mobile/interaction/)
- NN/g’s confirmation and error-prevention articles, cited above, add the interaction pattern: name the person and the consequence on the review, use a verb button, do not default the send action, and do not rely on a dialog people have learned to dismiss. Showing the form error beside the field is the recovery pattern. — [NN/g confirmation dialogs](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/confirmation-dialog/); [NN/g modal dialogs](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/modal-nonmodal-dialog/)

### Inferences

- Because a recorded crypto transfer is not reversible, 3.3.4 has to be met before that recording: check the amount and destination, show them for review, and let the sender change them. A cancel link after the recording does not satisfy “reversible,” and labeling it as undo would hide the limit.
- The review screen should repeat, in text, the sender’s total, the fees, the rate, the expected amount in the recipient’s currency, the recipient’s name, and the destination (bank account ending, phone, or pickup place). Those are the CFPB pre-payment facts plus the WCAG example of showing the address and amount before commit. The irreversibility sentence sits in that same block, not on a later help page.
- The send control should read “Send $110 to Carlos Gomez” / “Enviar $110 a Carlos Gomez,” not “Confirm” or “Sí.” Give it at least a 44 by 44 CSS pixel target if it is the irreversible control. Keep a separate, equally easy “Change amount” or “Change recipient” control so the review is a real correction step. Do not put Cancel and Send so close that a 24-pixel circle around each would overlap.
- Write the review at about a nine-year-school reading level in both languages, and keep a shorter one-sentence version above the detail: who receives it, what they are expected to get, and that it cannot be undone once recorded. Proper names such as the recipient and the bank can sit outside the readability count; jargon such as “on-chain,” “hash,” and “gas” cannot, unless a plain phrase is also there.
- When a check fails, name the field and the repair: “The account number has 9 digits. This one has 8. Change it before sending.” Do the same in Spanish. Do not clear the form. Do not use a red border as the only signal.
- When the status changes from recorded, to accepted by the payout company, to paid out, expose that change as a status message a screen reader can announce without moving focus. Do not rely on green, amber, or a spinner alone.
- At 200% text and at a narrow phone width, the recipient’s name and both amounts have to remain on screen without sideways scrolling. Use text, not an image of the amount, so resizing works.
- A second, unusual confirmation, such as retyping the amount, is justified by NN/g only for a rare high-stakes send. Using it on every family support payment will train people to tap through it. The everyday protection is the specific review, the large named button, and the check of the destination before the recording.

### Gaps

- WCAG 2.2 was not re-tested against a built screen. The criteria above are the requirements; they are not a conformance claim for any app.
- No measured reading grade of the CFPB model sentences was calculated for this note. The recommendation to stay near lower-secondary level is the AAA criterion applied to this audience, not a test score.
- ISO 24495-1:2023 was identified from the W3C reading-level page and was not read.
- Platform-specific rules for iOS Dynamic Type and Android font scale were not researched separately from WCAG resize and reflow.
